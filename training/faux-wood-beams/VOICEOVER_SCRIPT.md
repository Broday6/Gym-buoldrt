## 02 · What they are

**0:13.8** Ekena faux wood beams are high-density polyurethane, molded from real wood timbers, so every knot, check and saw mark is captured.

**0:23.2** Each beam is finished by hand, so color and undertones vary a little from batch to batch.

**0:36.2** The hollow core can hide wiring, pipes and lighting, or wrap a real structural beam. The faux beam itself is decorative, never structural.


## 03 · Why faux

**0:45.7** Unlike real timber, polyurethane will not rot, warp, crack or split, and insects cannot damage it.

**0:53.2** The beams are rated for interior and exterior use, and they carry a limited lifetime warranty.

## 04 · Four shapes

**1:10.2** Timberthane comes in four shapes. The 1-sided plank is a one-inch-thick face board that mounts flat.

**1:17.0** The 2-sided L-beam covers two faces, for corners like where a ceiling meets a wall.

**1:30.2** U and L beams mount over wood blocking cut to the beam's inside width. End caps are optional, to close off an open beam end.

## 10 · Installation

**4:41.4** Installation, from the Ekena installation guide.

**4:45.1** Let the beams acclimate in the room for at least 24 hours. Then snap a chalk line down the center of each beam's path.

**4:53.1** Cut 2 by 4 blocking to the beam's inside width. Fasten the first block 2 feet from the wall, then one every 3 to 6 feet.

**5:01.9** Measure every run, cut the beam a little long, and dry fit it before any adhesive.

**5:08.4** Apply construction adhesive to the outside of the blocking and the top edges of the beam, press it in place, then fasten with finishing nails or screws.

**5:18.1** Clean off extra adhesive and caulk where the beam meets the wall. Cover any splice with a flexible beam strap.
