## 02 · What they are

**0:13.8** Ekena faux wood beams are high-density polyurethane, molded from real wood timbers, so every knot, check and saw mark is captured.

**0:23.2** Each beam is finished by hand, so color and undertones vary a little from batch to batch.

**0:36.2** The hollow core can hide wiring, pipes and lighting, or wrap a real structural beam. The faux beam itself is decorative, never structural.


## 03 · Why faux

**0:45.7** Unlike real timber, polyurethane will not rot, warp, crack or split, and insects cannot damage it.

**0:53.2** They work inside and out. Outdoors, paint or finish them promptly, and keep them out of heat traps over 140 degrees.

**1:01.7** The warranty is a limited lifetime warranty for the original owner, pro-rated after the first three years.

**1:09.0** Ekena calls them a fraction of the cost and installation time of real wood, and they go up with standard woodworking tools.


## 04 · Four shapes

## 10 · Installation

**4:59.9** Installation, from Ekena's current installation guide for Heritage and Timberthane beams.

**5:06.3** Acclimate the beams in the room for at least 48 hours, laid flat. Then mark each beam line with a chalk line, laser or painter's tape.

**5:15.9** Cut wood blocks an eighth of an inch narrower than the inside of the beam, and fasten them at the beam ends and about every 2 to 3 feet.

**5:24.9** Screw the blocks into joists. Where there is no joist, use heavy-duty toggle bolts.

**5:30.7** Cut the beam an eighth of an inch longer than the opening and spring it into place. Use a hand saw: some beams hide fasteners.

**5:40.2** Run PL Premium adhesive on the top edges and blocks, then drive four trim screws per block, two per side. Fill gaps with colored caulk.
  *spoken as:* Run P L Premium adhesive on the top edges and blocks, then drive four trim screws per block, two per side. Fill gaps with colored caulk.

**5:50.1** Joining two beams? Ekena strongly recommends a beam strap over the splice.

**5:55.5** Light fixtures hang from blocking tied to a joist, through a hole cut in the beam, never from the beam itself.
